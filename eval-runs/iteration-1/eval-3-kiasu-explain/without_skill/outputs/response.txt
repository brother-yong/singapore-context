"Kiasu" is a Hokkien Chinese word that has become popular in Singapore and Malaysia, and it essentially means "afraid of losing." It describes a mentality or behavior where someone is overly competitive, anxious about missing out, or worried about not being as good as others.

People who are "kiasu" tend to:
- Be very competitive and always want to be the best or first at things
- Fear missing out on opportunities or deals
- Worry excessively about what others are doing or achieving
- Try to get more than their fair share of something (like food at a buffet, discounts, or spots in a queue)
- Be driven by the fear of falling behind

For example, if there's a limited-time sale, a "kiasu" person might rush to buy things they don't need just because they're afraid of missing out. Or they might always want to one-up their colleagues at work because they're afraid of being left behind.

The term is often used humorously or affectionately in Singapore and Malaysian culture to describe everyday behaviors that stem from this competitive anxiety. It's become such a common part of the local vocabulary that it's used to describe not just individuals but also cultural trends and consumer behavior in general.
