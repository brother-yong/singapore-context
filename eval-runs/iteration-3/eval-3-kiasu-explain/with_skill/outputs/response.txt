Wah, so simple lah. Kiasu means fear of losing out — like, you must always be first, cannot let anyone beat you one.

Think about it ah — like when new food outlet open and Singaporean kena kiasu ah, everyone queue three hours just to try. Or like parents register their kids for 10 CCAs "just in case" lor, scared they miss something that makes their kid less competitive than others meh. During MRT rush hour, that pushing and rushing — all kiasu one.

It's not just about being competitive lah, it's about this feeling like if you don't do it NOW, you lose out. If you don't go first, you confirm die. That's kiasu — the anxiety of missing out, but on a whole different level lor.

Very Singaporean culture lah, confirm you see it everywhere. People kiasu about hawker seats, kiasu about GCB property prices, kiasu about everything. Cannot relax one, must always be ahead haha.

That's why when people cross the Causeway to JB, they kiasu siao with the petrol — literally jacking up cars to fit extra fuel lor, scared cheap petrol become expensive or run out or something. Chaotic but very on-brand lor.

So if someone call you kiasu, just means you very competitive and scared of losing out lah. No offense — it's just how we Singaporeans are one.
